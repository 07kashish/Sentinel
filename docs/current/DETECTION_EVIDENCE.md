# Detection evidence

What the detection numbers in this repository are, what they are not, and
what a blind evaluation will add.

## What exists

| Artifact | What it measures | Corpus | Environment | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `evaluation/metrics.json` (regenerated by the `test` CI job, stamped with provenance) | Precision, recall and per-class counts of the deterministic policy | the repository's own labelled corpus under `evaluation/` | GitHub-hosted runner, Python 3.12 | The corpus was assembled by the people who tuned the rules. It measures fit, not generalisation. |
| `archive/pre-cleanup:docs/benign_tool_call_false_positives.json` | False-positive rate on benign tool calls | `evaluation/` benign set | development machine | Same authorship limitation. |
| `archive/pre-cleanup:docs/evidence/attack_campaign.json` | Outcome of the in-repo attack campaign against a running instance | `archive/pre-cleanup:scripts/run_attack_campaign.py` (removed in Phase 2) | development machine, SQLite | Attacks written by the defender. |

## What these numbers may be used for

- Regression control: a change that lowers them fails CI.
- Describing the detector's behaviour on the stated corpus, with the corpus,
  date, environment and this limitation stated in the same sentence.

## What they may not be used for

- Any statement about real-world detection rate, "catches X% of attacks", or
  comparison with another product. RISK R-005 stays open until a blind
  evaluation exists.

## Blind evaluation (protocol implemented 2026-09-20; no corpus sealed yet)

1. A corpus is assembled by someone who has not read `config/default_policy.json`,
   and its sha256 is committed under
   `docs/current/evidence/detection/` before any run.
2. `evaluation/run_blind.py` runs the shipped policy against it and writes
   per-class precision and recall with Wilson intervals, the corpus digest,
   the commit and the environment.
3. The result is cited here by digest. Until then this section is a plan, not evidence.

The protocol above is now `evaluation/run_blind.py` (`seal`, then `run`), tested by `tests_arch/test_blind_evaluation.py`. It refuses an unsealed or edited corpus, an uncommitted seal, a corpus under 50 cases or with one label, and a second run unless acknowledged, in which case the result records `"blind": false`. `evaluation/blind/` contains no seal, so this document still reports no blind evidence.
